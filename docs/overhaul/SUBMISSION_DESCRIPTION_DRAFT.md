## Project summary

Mars Outpost Survival is a space-themed STEM game where young players make the decisions that keep a small Mars expedition alive. Players manage electricity, oxygen, water and food, assign astronauts to construction, and restore damaged equipment through hands-on repair challenges.

The lesson comes from consequences: a farm uses power and water to produce food; life-support equipment needs electricity to keep operating; and batteries provide a limited reserve. Choosing what to build and what to keep running changes the colony's resources over time.

Repairs use different interactions for different equipment. Engineering skill makes the challenge more forgiving. A failed repair leaves the equipment broken and unavailable for another simulated day, making backup capacity valuable.

## Optional: include only after dust-storm verification

A seeded random dust storm reduces solar electricity and asks the player to choose between maintaining farm production and conserving power for life support. The game shows the effect on batteries and equipment, then restores normal conditions when the storm clears. The event makes the same colony play differently across sessions.

## NASA science and game assumptions

The reference colony uses crew-consumption inputs documented in the project's `SCIENCE.md`, including NASA's Baseline Values and Assumptions Document and NASA's Mars24 calendar reference. The included data is static; the game does not claim a live NASA weather feed.

Equipment ratings, crop output, repair challenges, injury recovery and compressed event timing are gameplay assumptions. The prototype is designed to illustrate resource and engineering tradeoffs; it is not a validated prediction of astronaut health or Mars habitat performance.

Judges can trace individual model inputs and source links in `SCIENCE.md` and `docs/A3_RESEARCH_AND_SOLUTION.md`. Include those files with a source-project submission or provide access through the project repository.
